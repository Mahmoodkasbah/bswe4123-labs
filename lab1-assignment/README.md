**Timeout:** I chose a timeout of 1.0 seconds because it is bigger than the normal fast response time (~2–6 ms) and bigger than the slow service delay (0.8 s / ~803 ms). If the timeout were 0.5 s, then service 8003 (~803 ms) would be marked DOWN instead of SLOW. If it were 10 s, then it would take way too long to notice that a stopped service is actually down.

**Number of checks:** I chose 3 failed checks in a row. In my test, 8001 became SUSPECT at round 13 (19:46:31) and DOWN at round 15 (19:46:39), so detection took about 8 seconds. With only 1 check, I would gain faster detection the moment a failure happens but lose stability because even one brief hiccup would mark it DOWN. With 10 checks, I would gain fewer false alarms from temporary glitches but lose quick detection since a dead service would stay undetected much longer.

The counter goes back to 0 after one success because a successful response means the service is answering again, so the streak of consecutive failures is broken.
